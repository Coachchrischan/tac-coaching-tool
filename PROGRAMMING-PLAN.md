## Block 1 — Strength-Hypertrophy (10 weeks)

**Main lifts**
- Week 1: top-set triple (work up to a 3RM), then two back-off sets of 7 at ~75%.
  Doubles as testing: capture the 3RM (or calculated 1RM) as testing data.
- Wave loading thereafter: sets of 9, sets of 7, sets of 5 at varying percentages,
  three cycles across the block.
- Week 7: mid-block deload (breaks up the volume ramp).
- Weeks 8-10: final ascending push to finish strong.

**Accessories**
- Ascending volume within each three-week wave (climb weeks 2-4, small dip, climb
  through the 5-6-7 region, then 8-9-10), with the week 7 deload interrupting the middle.

## Week 11 — Deload / skills week

- Doubles as a skills/workshop week teaching technique for the coming strength block.
- Example: deadlift workshop to prep the strength phase.

## Block 2 — Strength (6 weeks, weeks 12-17)

**Main lifts**: two mini wave cycles instead of full wave loading.
- Cycle 1 (3 weeks): 7-5-3
- Cycle 2 (3 weeks): 5-3-1

**Accessories**: descending volume with increasing intensity; bigger emphasis on
strength accessories given the heavier main focus.

**End-of-block retest**: a sneaky testing session in Christmas week, one class testing
all compounds trained through the block. Proves the block worked before the break.

## Christmas — two-week gap

Detraining window (flagged by the roundtable): consider a maintenance option or a
return-to-training ramp in the first January week.

## Block 3 — Unilateral muscle-endurance (4 weeks, new year)

- Week 1: unilateral testing to get everyone sorted (lots of newcomers expected).
- Predominantly single-limb exercises; lots of tempo, time under tension, asymmetric holds.
- Higher-rep muscle-endurance focus, addressing imbalances at the start of the year.

## Block 4 — Hypertrophy (12 weeks)

- Aesthetic-based goals focus. Main strength movements still retained.
- Then back into the strength-hypertrophy / strength cycle.

## Coverage / variety tracking (all blocks)

Two tracking layers run side by side:
1. **Movement pattern** (shoulder rotation, hinge, squat, push, pull, ...)
2. **Muscle groups**, basic anatomical level (gastrocs, soleus, quads, hamstrings, ...)

Plus **trunk categories** to tick off: lower abs, rectus abdominis, rotation,
anti-rotation, anti-flexion, anti-extension.

**Coverage rule:** everything on both lists must be hit at least once across any two
consecutive phases (a rolling 20-24 week window), not within a single block.

## Standing principles

- Scaling options exist for every exercise (already handled in the tool).
- **Anchor lifts stay stable across blocks** so members can see progress; variety is
  managed via the accessory slots.
- Follow-up: discuss accessory variety management with Dave.

## Roundtable review — expert notes

- **S&C coach**: intensity ramp across the year (9-7-5 → 7-5-3 → 5-3-1) is clean.
  Flag: little visible conditioning/aerobic work for a partly-CrossFit crowd; confirm
  whether it is parked elsewhere (ESD lane?).
- **Hypertrophy specialist**: likes the ascending-volume accessory logic and the
  dedicated 12-week block; long-ramp concern addressed by the week 7 deload.
- **Physiotherapist**: values the unilateral block and the coverage matrix as an
  injury-risk audit. Suggestion: thread a little single-limb work through other blocks
  rather than isolating it to one phase.
- **Periodisation researcher**: macro shape respects phase potentiation. Flag: the
  Christmas gap is a detraining window; consider maintenance or a January re-entry
  ramp. End-of-strength-block retest now added.
- **Operations**: coherent and ambitious; delivery risk is the main issue. Keep coach
  notes clear, the coverage matrix visible to all coaches, and anchor lifts stable.
